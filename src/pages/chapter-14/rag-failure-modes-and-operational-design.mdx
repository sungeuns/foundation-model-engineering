# 14.6 RAG Failure Modes and Operational Design

“The assistant cited our refund policy and still gave the wrong answer.” Replacing the embedding model is tempting, but the citation alone does not tell you what failed. The retriever may have found an obsolete policy, the context builder may have dropped an exception, or the generator may have ignored a passage it received.

RAG combines a generator with retrieved information [[1]](#ref-1). To debug that combination, follow one answer all the way back to the source version. The worked incident below is fictional; its documents, counts, and deadlines are teaching examples, not a production benchmark.

## 1. Follow One Incorrect Answer

A customer asks: **“I renewed my annual plan yesterday. Can I get a refund?”** For this example, the authoritative policy says that first purchases have a 30-day window, while renewals have a seven-day window.

| Stage | Recorded evidence | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Policy v3 contains both windows and explicitly supersedes v2 | An authoritative answer exists |
| Candidate retrieval | Top 20 includes v3's renewal clause and v2's old 30-day rule | Candidate retrieval found the needed evidence |
| Ranking | The renewal clause is ranked eighth | It may still be lost before generation |
| Context construction | Only the top five passages are sent; v2 survives, v3's renewal clause does not | The generator never received the decisive passage |
| Answer | “You have 30 days,” with a valid link to v2 | The citation exists, but the answer uses a superseded rule |

There are two defects here: a lifecycle filter allowed a superseded source, and the evidence needed for this question did not survive context selection. Improving recall alone cannot repair either observation. Keep document version, chunk identifier, candidate rank, final context position, and truncation reason in the trace. Log identifiers and controlled excerpts rather than copying sensitive documents into general application logs.

This also explains why adding more context is not automatically the fix. *Lost in the Middle* found position-dependent performance on its evaluated long-context tasks [[2]](#ref-2). Test evidence placement and distractors for your model; do not turn that paper into a rule that every model ignores every middle passage.

## 2. Use Interventions to Locate the Failure

Start with a small, human-labeled set of answerable and unanswerable questions. For each answerable question, label the authoritative evidence needed for the answer, including exceptions. Then rerun the same generator under controlled inputs:

| Experiment | Keep fixed | Replace | Interpretation if the answer improves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reference-evidence run | Question, generator, prompt, decoding | Supply the labeled authoritative passage | Evidence delivery is a bottleneck; inspect retrieval and context construction |
| Candidate-retention run | Retrieved top 20, generator | Ensure required passages survive the context budget | Ranking, packing, or truncation is implicated |
| Source-version run | Question and retrieval settings | Remove superseded versions before retrieval | Source lifecycle policy is implicated |
| Evidence-only check | Authoritative passage and question | Human or executable answer check | Establish whether the task is answerable from that evidence at all |

These are diagnostic interventions, not independent scores to add together. Several defects can coexist. A reference-evidence run that still fails suggests examining the prompt, generation, ambiguous question, or labeling before rebuilding the index.

Measure the stage boundaries explicitly. In an **authored 100-question example**, suppose required evidence is found for 90 questions, survives the context budget for 72, and leads to a supported correct answer for 60. The question-level evidence hit rate is 90%; evidence retention among hits is 72/90 = 80%; supported-answer success among retained cases is 60/72 ≈ 83.3%. End-to-end success is 60%, not 90%. Those nested counts assume these are the only successful cases; they are not a universal factorization of RAG accuracy. Multi-document tasks also need coverage of the *full* required evidence set, not just one relevant chunk.

## 3. A Similarity Score Is Not an Answer Probability

A cosine similarity of 0.82 does not mean an answer has an 82% chance of being correct. Score distributions change with the embedding model, corpus, query type, and candidate filtering. Rankers may also emit uncalibrated logits. Choose any threshold using labeled examples and the cost of a wrong answer, then measure the resulting coverage and error rate on a separate holdout.

For the refund question, checking the transaction type and policy version matters more than a universal similarity threshold. Ask for clarification if “purchase” could mean first purchase or renewal. Query the authorized transaction system when the answer depends on the customer's actual renewal date. If no current policy can be verified, say that the policy cannot be confirmed instead of substituting the model's general knowledge.

Self-RAG learns retrieval and critique decisions using reflection tokens [[3]](#ref-3); CRAG evaluates retrieved evidence and selects corrective actions [[4]](#ref-4). These are research designs for controlling evidence use. They do not supply calibrated thresholds, access permissions, or an unlimited retry budget for your application.

## 4. Treat Timeout Fallbacks as Separate Behaviors

Suppose an illustrative service reserves 1,000 ms before generation: 200 ms for retrieval, 250 ms for reranking, 150 ms for version/access checks and context construction, and 400 ms of slack. These are assigned budgets, not measurements. Enforce one request deadline; a second retrieval cannot restart the clock.

If the reranker times out, using first-stage order is acceptable only if that degraded path has passed its own evidence and answer-quality checks. A smaller candidate set can discard the one passage that matters. Preserve authorization and source-version filters on every path. If current evidence or required checks are unavailable, return an explicit limitation or route to a human. “The answer may be stale” is not a sufficient fallback for a definitive policy decision.

Track fallback frequency, evidence retention, unsupported-answer rate, abstention, and tail latency separately for normal and degraded paths. A fast fallback that confidently invents an answer is not a successful request.

## 5. Check Claims, Not Just Citation Markers

For each material claim, ask two questions: **does the cited passage support it, and is that passage authoritative for this question?** In the example, v2 supports a 30-day statement but fails the second question. A link checker would pass it; an evidence check should not.

Maintain separate measures for citation coverage (which claims have citations), citation support (whether the cited text entails the claim), and source validity (version, scope, and access). Treat retrieved text as data: an instruction inside a document must not change the assistant's permissions or evaluation rules. Use deterministic checks for dates and identifiers where possible, with human-reviewed samples for semantic support.

## 6. Make the Repair Reviewable

A repair should include the failing question, allowed source versions, expected evidence, the old and new context traces, and the resulting answer. Keep related questions and source revisions together when splitting evaluation data so paraphrases of the incident do not become a misleading “unseen” release set.

Compare the change on a sealed set containing renewals, first purchases, missing dates, conflicting versions, unauthorized documents, and deliberately unavailable evidence. Record accuracy, supported answers, abstention coverage, critical errors, latency, and cost by slice. Apply predeclared release thresholds with uncertainty as described in [production evaluation and release gates](/chapter-17/production-evaluation-and-release-gates). Retain the previous index snapshot, ingestion/filter configuration, retriever/reranker versions, and prompt/model bundle so rollback restores one coherent pipeline.

The practical debugging question is: **at which boundary did the required evidence disappear or become invalid?** Answering it usually produces a more specific change than “use a better model.”

## Quizzes

<details>
<summary>Quiz 1: The correct clause appears in the top 20 but not in the final top five. Which change should you test before replacing the embedding model?</summary>
*Inspect ranking, packing, and truncation, then run a candidate-retention intervention with the same retrieved set. The trace already shows that first-stage retrieval found the clause; also fix any source-version filtering defect.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 2: Why is a cosine score of 0.82 insufficient to authorize a policy answer?</summary>
*It is a similarity score, not a calibrated probability of answer correctness. The passage can be stale, scoped to a different transaction, or incomplete. Validate source authority and measure the threshold against labeled cases and decision costs.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 3: In the 100-question example, why is 90% evidence retrieval not 90% answer success?</summary>
*Only 72 cases retain the needed evidence, and only 60 produce a supported correct answer. The stage-conditioned rates are 80% retention and about 83.3% generation success; end-to-end success is 60% under the example's stated assumptions.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 4: Is continuing without a reranker always safer than returning an error?</summary>
*No. The fallback changes the evidence supplied to the generator and can exclude a decisive exception. Validate it separately, preserve access and version checks, and abstain or escalate if its requirements fail.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 5: Can a citation be textually supported and still produce a wrong answer?</summary>
*Yes. An obsolete or out-of-scope policy may support the quoted statement while being invalid for the user's case. Citation support and source validity are separate checks.*
</details>
