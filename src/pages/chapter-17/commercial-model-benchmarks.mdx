# 17.6 Commercial and Open-weight Model Benchmarks

A new release changes more than a name on a leaderboard. It may change the downloadable artifact, license, prompt encoding, reasoning budget, price tier, or revision behind an API alias. Start with those contracts, then measure the complete workflow.

**As of 2026-11-15.** This static snapshot covers 32 selected profiles, not every release. Dates identify an explicit launch, weight-publication milestone or checked artifact revision; they are not uniformly first-launch dates. A public repository's creation date alone does not establish when its weights became available. Reverify the linked sources before a later publication or deployment.

## 1. A New Benchmark Version Is a New Measurement

Artificial Analysis now reports **Intelligence Index v5.0.1**, with nine evaluations spanning knowledge work, coding, science and general tasks [[1]](#ref-1). This chapter replaces its October v4.3.2 values throughout. A score that changes between those versions is not evidence that the same weights improved or deteriorated. Do not subtract the old score from the new score as a performance trend.

The evaluated system includes effort, fallback, scaffold, tool access, token/context budgets, sampling and grader. These profiles are not equal-compute experiments. An index is not an accuracy percentage; a context limit is not retrieval accuracy. Missing values mean **not reported under this mapped revision and methodology**, never zero.

## 3. What the New Releases Teach

### Hosted access and downloadable weights are separate milestones

**Kimi K3.1 Pro** now has public weight files. Its card discloses 2.8T total and 104B active parameters, a KDA/gated-sparse-attention hybrid, and quantization-aware formats. The table uses the checked November 5 artifact revision. Those disclosures replace the earlier “weights planned” boundary; they do not reveal the complete training mixture or prove hosted/local equivalence. Highspeed remains a separate hosted profile without borrowed specifications [[49]](#ref-49) [[50]](#ref-50) [[51]](#ref-51) [[52]](#ref-52).

**Mistral Large 4** has public weights after its October preview. Its model card and current documentation establish a granular multimodal MoE; the custom Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight License places an additional agreement requirement on Model-as-a-Service businesses. Open weight therefore does not mean unrestricted hosting. The current AA high-effort profile is distinct from the old preview result [[24]](#ref-24) [[25]](#ref-25) [[26]](#ref-26) [[27]](#ref-27).

**Inkling 2 Small** followed its hosted launch with public Apache 2.0 weights. The card reports 594B total/17B active parameters and mixed MXFP8/NVFP4 artifacts. The vLLM integration describes a native draft head and hardware-specific optimizations. Active compute and packed payload do not include all resident weights, KV state, vision work or temporary buffers; draft-head speed must be measured on the actual deployment [[65]](#ref-65) [[66]](#ref-66) [[67]](#ref-67) [[68]](#ref-68).

### General-purpose and restricted configurations need different evaluations

**GPT-6.1 Terra** adds another text/image-to-text operating point. The official documentation separates its 1,050,000-token context window, 128,000-token output limit and supported reasoning levels; tool calling requires Responses. Its standard rates are \$1 input/\$5 output per million tokens, with separate cache, long-context and service-tier conditions. Architecture, parameters and training mixture remain undisclosed [[60]](#ref-60) [[61]](#ref-61).

**Claude Fable 5.2** and **Mythos 5.2** share an underlying model according to Anthropic, with different safeguards and access. Mythos requires verification-program access. This table does not transfer Fable's measured score to Mythos. Fable's standard \$8/\$40 input/output rates differ from its \$16/\$80 fast mode; task cost also depends on reasoning length and fallback. Provider demonstrations of long tasks are evidence about those tested systems, not general reliability guarantees [[62]](#ref-62) [[63]](#ref-63) [[64]](#ref-64).

### Read the parameter accounting, not just the family label

The **Qwen3.8 Flash Next** card separates the 125B main model/6B active parameters from 51B n-gram embeddings and 4B MTP parameters. It combines Gated DeltaNet, block-sparse QSA and gated residual streams. Its native 262,144 context is distinct from an extended configuration and the hosted Flash service. The November 7 date is a checked revision, not a new-launch claim [[69]](#ref-69) [[70]](#ref-70) [[71]](#ref-71).

**GLM-5.3 Flash** is a separately trained multimodal base, not merely GLM-5.3 with a faster serving setting. The card reports 320B total/18B active parameters and MIT weights. Its October 15 revision is included for architectural coverage, not presented as a November launch [[72]](#ref-72) [[73]](#ref-73) [[74]](#ref-74). Smaller or domain-specific entries elsewhere in the table likewise cannot inherit another family member's benchmark or architecture.

## 5. Turn a Shortlist into a Release Decision

Use the quality/cost/latency/context/openness/safety Pareto surface rather than choosing one universal winner. Evaluate the same private tasks with pinned tools, permissions, scaffold, effort, retries, input/output limits and grader. Report paired uncertainty, language/domain slices, base-capability retention, format/tool correctness, safety, tail latency, error rate and cost per successful workflow. A critical slice failure blocks release even if the mean improves.

Keep weights/API revision, tokenizer/template, quantization, runtime, generation configuration, tool schema and safety policy in one immutable artifact bundle. Test hosted and local versions separately. Canary with bounded permissions, retain a known-good route and rehearse rollback of cached state and pending effects. Typed decision models need a separate calibration/selective-risk evaluation; see [Section 16.7](/chapter-16/decision-models-and-selective-prediction).

## Quizzes

<details>
<summary>Quiz 1: Why can an unchanged model receive a lower index after this update?</summary>
*The evaluation changed from v4.3.2 to v5.0.1. Different tasks and aggregation define a different measurement; the difference is not a controlled estimate of model regression.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 2: What changed the Kimi and Mistral weight-status labels?</summary>
*Verified public weight artifacts, rather than a promised date, moved the claim boundary. Their licenses, revisions and local serving conditions still require separate checks.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 3: Why does Mythos not inherit the Fable score?</summary>
*Access, safeguards and evaluated serving configuration are part of the system. Shared underlying weights do not make two evaluated systems interchangeable.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 4: Why is the public Ling checkpoint left without a current index?</summary>
*The current profile identifies a 1019 variant whose equivalence is unverified. A similarly named result cannot fill a revision-specific gap.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 5: Why can a cheap token rate produce a costly completed task?</summary>
*Reasoning length, retries, caching, tool calls, latency and failure rates all contribute. Compare matched complete workflows, not token rates alone.*
</details>
