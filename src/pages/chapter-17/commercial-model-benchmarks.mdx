# 17.6 Commercial and Open-weight Model Benchmarks

A model release changes more than a leaderboard. It can change access, licensing, prompt encoding, pricing, output budgets, or the revision behind a stable API alias. Evaluate those contracts before treating a familiar name as a drop-in replacement.

**As of 2026-10-25.** This is a dated historical snapshot, not a live leaderboard. Release sources and AA profiles were rechecked for this pass and **must be reverified** before a later publication or procurement. Dates are selected milestones: announcement, broader access, weight publication, or an explicitly identified artifact revision. They are not all initial launch dates.

## 1. Read the Evaluated System

The index uses Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index **v4.3.2**, comprising ten evaluations [[1]](#ref-1). Effort, fallback, scaffold, tools, sampling, context, grader, and revision are part of a result. Different profiles are not equal-compute or controlled weights-only comparisons. An index is not a percentage, and a declared context limit is not long-context reasoning accuracy.

The table is a selection of 26 profiles, not a complete release catalog. Where AA does not identify effort, it remains not reported. New checkpoints do not inherit their base model's score, and missing prices do not mean free. Candidate selection should use the quality/cost/latency/context/openness/safety Pareto surface and a private evaluation under matched operational constraints.

## 3. What the October 19–25 Releases Change

### API availability and weights are different events

MiniMax M3.1 Flash launched through hosted products on October 19; public weight files are now present in its official repository, with the October 23 revision recorded separately. The card describes all-layer sparse attention, quantization-aware training and a DSpark draft head, under the MiniMax Community License [[44]](#ref-44) [[45]](#ref-45). The card reports approximately 428B total and 23B active parameters: a Hub's packed tensor count is not a substitute for architectural parameter count. Its standard launch rates are \$0.15 input/\$0.60 output per million tokens; cache hits are a separate rate. Check the serving recipe rather than treating the draft head as a universal speed multiplier.

Kimi K3.1 Pro and Highspeed are usable through hosted channels. The October 24 technical post promises full Pro weights **by November 4**; no separate Highspeed weight artifact is verified, so neither is labeled released/open-weight here [[48]](#ref-48). “3T-class” is the provider's description; exact total/active counts and the full training mixture are undisclosed in the post. A hosted score does not resolve those gaps. Highspeed's claimed 2–3× speed is not transferred into a cross-provider latency plot. Its separately unverified context limit, price and index remain not reported.

Mistral Large 4 still has a preview/API contract and later-October weight plan [[24]](#ref-24). A promised date becoming close is not evidence that weights have arrived. Conversely, Step 5 **Preview** has public weights: the name does not determine release state [[39]](#ref-39).

The rechecked Mistral changelog still labels Large 4 Public Preview and says open weights are coming soon [[61]](#ref-61).

### Pin the revision behind the familiar name

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 now has its own AA max-effort profile: index 45 and \$0.26 observed task cost [[43]](#ref-43). These are independently recorded for the named revision, not borrowed from September. Its mutable API alias and protocol-aware encoding still require migration checks [[42]](#ref-42).

Ling 3.1 Flash's public MIT card specifies a hybrid KDA/gated-MLA stack and a 560B-total/25B-active MoE [[50]](#ref-50). The table's October 20 date is the checked artifact revision, not a claim about its first hosted release. AA also exposes a distinct **1019 hosted profile**; its relationship to this public checkpoint is unverified, so this row retains the explicitly matched original profile's 41 and \$1.01 [[51]](#ref-51). A higher score under a similar name is insufficient revision evidence.

### Small memory targets describe a particular artifact

NVIDIA's October 23 Bolt E4B 4GB release derives from Gemma-4-E4B, unlike the hybrid Mamba/MoE Bolt 74B. Its card specifies 8B total/4B effective parameters, OpenMDW-1.1, and separate up-to-64K input and output limits [[58]](#ref-58) [[59]](#ref-59). Do not sum those into a verified unified window or infer its architecture from the family name. The “4GB” target does not reserve memory for arbitrary context, concurrency, or a voice pipeline.

Smaug Nemotron 3.5 NVFP4 is an OpenMDW-1.1 fine-tune with public weight files. Abacus describes adaptation that accounts for rounding into the served format [[55]](#ref-55) [[56]](#ref-56). This motivates testing the **merged quantized artifact**, not just the floating-point adapter. Provider-reported gains and base-model results are not a revision-specific AA score. Section 13.5 covers state and workspace accounting beyond nominal weight bits.

Ling 3.0 Flash Sante is an MIT healthcare research checkpoint; its card reports 124B total/5.1B active and domain evaluations [[53]](#ref-53). Those results are not clinical validation or evidence of a universal general-purpose gain. Use domain-specific review and an appropriate task baseline rather than manufacture an AA value from medical benchmarks.

## 4. Explore Profiles Without Filling Missing Values

Only rows with both plotted values appear for a metric/axis/filter combination; the alphabetical selector is not a universal ranking. Token-price points use the illustrative proxy $(3p_{in}+p_{out})/4$, with uncached standard input/output rates per million tokens. It excludes cache writes/hits, tools, modalities, long-context tiers and output-length differences. Missing values remain not reported, including empty company/axis combinations.

The context metric preserves provider-specific semantics. For Bolt E4B it is the declared input limit; Gemini 4 Argon's contract uses a unified window. Promotional prices also have expiry: Google's documented introductory Argon rates run through November 30 [[17]](#ref-17) [[18]](#ref-18). No incomparable speed measurements are combined into a fabricated curve.

## 5. Evaluate the Pipeline and Its Decision Boundaries

Jev, OpenAI Decisions, open d1 and LemonDecider expose bounded decisions rather than free-form answer generation. Their option probabilities, calibration, and selective risk need their own evaluation; a Decision Index result is not an AA generative index point. [Section 16.7](/chapter-16/decision-models-and-selective-prediction) explains their disclosed mechanisms, abstention policies, and release gates.

For generation or decisions, evaluate the same private tasks with pinned scaffold, tool permissions, effort, input/output budget, retries, and grader. Include language/domain, base-capability retention, format/tool, safety, latency-tail, review capacity, cost per successful workflow, and error-rate slices. Test local quantizations independently of hosted profiles. Use paired uncertainty or justified sample sizes; critical-slice failure blocks release even if the average improves.

Keep weights/API revision, tokenizer/encoding, template, calibration and decision policy, runtime, tool schema, generation configuration, and safety policy hashes in one artifact bundle. Canary under bounded permissions, retain a known-good route, and rehearse rollback with cached state and pending effects. A new release is a reason to evaluate the complete system.

*The provider promises full weights by November 4. Hosted access and a benchmark record do not establish public weight availability as of October 25.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 2: Why can DeepSeek 1017 now have an index while Smaug remains unscored?</summary>
*A separate revision-specific AA profile now exists for 1017. Smaug is a different fine-tune and cannot inherit its base model’s index.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 3: Why is Bolt E4B not assigned Bolt 74B’s architecture?</summary>
*The E4B card identifies a Gemma-derived design, while the 74B card describes a different hybrid architecture. Family branding is not an architecture specification.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 4: Why not copy Ling 1019’s higher score to the public Ling checkpoint?</summary>
*The hosted profile and public weight revision have not been established as equivalent. Similar naming is insufficient provenance.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 5: Why are decision models outside this index chart?</summary>
*Their typed outputs, calibration and selective risk require compatible task-specific evaluation. Different benchmark indexes cannot be combined as if they measured the same quantity.*
</details>
