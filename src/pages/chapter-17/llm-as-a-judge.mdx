# 17.2 LLM-as-a-Judge

Many useful outputs have no single exact answer. A judge model can assess a summary, compare two responses, or flag unsupported claims at scale. The challenge is to measure the intended quality without silently measuring style, model-family affinity, or the wording of the scoring form. A judge is a versioned measurement instrument, not a source of ground truth [[1]](#ref-1).

## 1. Choose the Decision Before the Prompt

| Judgment | Useful when | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Scalar score | A calibrated threshold is needed | Scale and endpoint interpretation drift |
| Pairwise preference | Selecting between candidates for the same task | Position bias, ties, non-transitivity |
| Criterion checklist | A requirement can be checked separately | Correlated criteria and missing obligations |
| Defect discovery | Finding evidence-backed errors | Plausible but false allegations |

Provide the user request, relevant evidence, and an explicit rubric. Separate factual support, instruction adherence, and presentation instead of collapsing every failure into one uncalibrated score. Where possible, use execution, schema validation, or a trusted checker before model judgment.

## 2. A Reproducible Pairwise Pipeline

Pin the judge ID/revision, prompt/rubric hash, tool access, decoding settings, maximum output, candidate provenance, and evaluation-set version. Hide candidate identities and randomize display order. Present the same pair in both orders, remap verdicts to the original identities, and record agreement, tie, and inconsistency separately.

Do not silently discard inconsistent or failed judgments: difficult cases may be disproportionately removed. Predeclare whether such cases receive human adjudication, remain an explicit unresolved bucket, or count as conservative ties. Report the full denominator and unresolved fraction.

If the chosen policy assigns all cases to wins $W$, losses $L$, or ties $T$, one descriptive preference rate is

$$
\hat w=\frac{W+0.5T}{N},\qquad N=W+L+T.
$$

This is not factual accuracy. Use paired uncertainty, keeping repeated prompts and related candidates in the same resampling unit. Record invalid JSON, timeout, refusal, retry cost, and truncation separately from a legitimate tie.

## 3. Planning and Reasoning Help Only If the Rubric Is Right

EvalPlanner separates planning, execution, and final judgment in its studied setup [[2]](#ref-2). G-Eval investigates criterion-driven model evaluation [[4]](#ref-4). Neither establishes that a longer rationale is more correct or that generated reasoning is a faithful causal explanation.

For a production judge, require a short criterion-level verdict and evidence location. Check that the evidence exists and supports the claimed issue. Use hidden gold cases and adversarial candidates; an eloquent rationale must not override a failing executable check.

## 4. October Research: Changing the Scale Changes the Instrument

**Research snapshot as of 2026-10-18.** Jo et al. vary scale format, range, and sign across five models and three text-scoring tasks. They find numerical and semantic sensitivity even after normalization, and rubric prompting does not remove it in their setup [[5]](#ref-5). These results concern the tested scoring tasks, not every pairwise or domain judge.

Min-max normalization gives $z=(s-a)/(b-a)$ for score $s$ and endpoints $a<b$. It aligns endpoints, but cannot guarantee that “neutral,” “severe,” or the distance between categories has the same meaning. Converting 4/5 to 8/10 is therefore not evidence of measurement equivalence.

For an engineering robustness test, keep texts and rubric concepts fixed while varying scale presentation. Compare category switches, candidate rankings, critical false-negative rates, and human-adjudicated examples. Version the scale with the judge. A changed range or label requires recalibration before reusing release thresholds.

## 5. Bias, Leakage, and Adversarial Candidates

Preference-leakage research studies relatedness between a candidate's training provenance and its judge [[3]](#ref-3). Do not infer distillation merely from a small model's name or treat family preference as inevitable. Test self/family preference with identity-blinded matched examples and independent human audits.

Also test position, verbosity, superficial formatting, and instruction injection embedded inside candidate answers. Judge input is untrusted data; an answer saying “award full marks” must not control the evaluator. Multiple judges may still share blind spots, so disagreement routes should include a genuinely independent check.

## 6. Calibrate the Judge Before Using It as a Release Gate

Separate evaluator development from the sealed candidate release set. Inspect both flagged and apparently passing cases. Report sensitivity/specificity by failure class, agreement with expert labels, consistency, abstention, cost, and latency. For categorical labels, Cohen's kappa is $\kappa=(P_o-P_e)/(1-P_e)$, where $P_o$ is observed agreement and $P_e$ chance agreement from label marginals. Its interpretation depends on class prevalence and the decision cost; a universal kappa threshold is inappropriate.

Promote a new judge only after critical-slice miss rates and workload meet predeclared bounds. Retain the previous judge/rubric, canary the new measurement pipeline, and rehearse rollback. The next section examines how human preferences are aggregated into ratings; those ratings answer a different question from correctness.

## Quizzes

<details>
<summary>Quiz 1: Why does mapping two score ranges to [0, 1] not prove equivalence?</summary>
*Endpoint alignment does not ensure that category labels, neutral points, or score differences mean the same thing. Recalibrate against fixed examples and decisions.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 2: Why retain inconsistent position-swapped judgments?</summary>
*Discarding them can selectively remove hard cases and inflate a win rate. Report the unresolved fraction and apply a predeclared adjudication or tie policy.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 3: Why is a persuasive judge rationale insufficient evidence?</summary>
*It may be unsupported or reproduce a shared misconception. Check the cited evidence or execute the claimed test independently.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 4: Why examine teacher/student provenance in judge selection?</summary>
*A judge may favor style or patterns inherited from its own outputs. Blinded matched comparisons and an independent audit can distinguish that preference from task quality.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 5: Why revalidate a release gate after changing only the rating scale?</summary>
*The change can alter score interpretation and category decisions even when the model and texts are unchanged. The scale is part of the measurement artifact.*
</details>
