# 18.4 Scalable Oversight

When a system can produce more code, proofs, or analyses than a reviewer can inspect, generation becomes cheap while verification stays expensive. **Scalable oversight** tries to extend a human's ability to discover and adjudicate important errors. It does not mean that agreement among models proves correctness or that a critic grants permission to execute an action.

## 1. Separate Discovery, Verification, and Authority

A useful analogy is a technical audit. One person locates suspicious claims, another reproduces the evidence, and an accountable owner decides whether the remaining uncertainty is acceptable. AI can assist each stage, but the stages need different contracts [[1]](#ref-1).

| Stage | Required output | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Propose | Candidate answer, change, or plan | Correctness |
| Critique | Specific claim, location, and alleged failure | A verified defect |
| Verify | Reproduction, proof obligation, or source evidence | Permission for a consequential action |
| Adjudicate | Accept/reject/defer decision and owner | Absence of unexamined defects |

## 2. Research Directions and Their Limits

AI-assisted critique studies whether a model helps people find flaws [[3]](#ref-3). Two critics can share a blind spot, so critique count is not independent evidence. Debate proposes adversarial argument as an aid to a judge [[2]](#ref-2); it relies on what the judge can verify and can reward persuasion rather than truth.

Weak-to-strong generalization experiments show that a stronger pretrained model can exceed a weak supervisor on studied tasks, while still falling short of its full supervised capability [[4]](#ref-4). The result does not prove that a model learned human intent or solve alignment for more capable systems. Iterative supervision can propagate an evaluator's errors unless external validation remains independent.

## 3. October Case: Review Orchestration Rather Than a Single Verdict

**Research snapshot as of 2026-10-18.** Google announced public Deep Review on October 14 as an evolution of its Paper Assistant Tool (PAT) [[5]](#ref-5). The earlier PAT paper describes segmentation, compute allocation, specialist verification, and grounded synthesis. Its mathematical-error study uses a selected 26-paper, 29-error subset and a grader audited by the authors [[6]](#ref-6). That evidence does not establish complete review coverage, general scientific validity, or a universal detection rate.

The engineering lesson is to make coverage and evidence explicit. Segment by claims and dependencies, retain full-document context where needed, allocate effort to uncertain claims, and deduplicate findings before human review. Search can check whether a citation exists; it cannot by itself establish that the cited result proves the claim. Re-run executable checks in an isolated environment and record failed verification attempts as well as confirmed defects.

## 4. Measure the Reviewer, Including What It Misses

For a labeled evaluation, let $TP$, $FP$, and $FN$ count correctly detected defects, false allegations, and missed defects. Then

$$
\operatorname{precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP},\qquad
\operatorname{recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}.
$$

Report undefined denominators as not estimable, not zero. Use seeded errors **and** independently confirmed natural errors. Measure reviewer time per confirmed critical defect, coverage by defect class, false-positive burden, abstention, latency, and cost. More sampling can improve discovery while overwhelming the reviewer with false allegations; jointly assess precision, recall, and review budget.

Human auditors must inspect a sample of apparently clean outputs to estimate misses. If they review only the critic's findings, recall cannot be estimated. Separate evaluator development from the sealed release set and record model, prompt, tool, and rubric versions.

## 5. Operating Contract and Failure Modes

Each finding should preserve the original artifact hash, exact location, evidence, reproduction command or proof obligation, severity rationale, uncertainty, verifier identity, and owner. Treat documents and tool outputs as untrusted input; a document cannot instruct its reviewer to suppress findings or retrieve secrets.

Use bounded critique/refine rounds. Test for shared blind spots, self-preference, unsupported citations, prompt injection, and loss of a valid finding during synthesis. An action executor separately checks authorization, scope, and idempotency. A critic's approval must never elevate permissions.

For rollout, compare the proposed oversight workflow with a human-only or established baseline on the same artifacts. Gate on critical-slice misses and reviewer workload as well as aggregate agreement. Canary changes, retain a known-good workflow, and rehearse rollback. The following chapter investigates internal mechanisms as an additional source of evidence; partial interpretability does not certify safety.

## Quizzes

<details>
<summary>Quiz 1: Why is agreement between two critics insufficient evidence that a defect is real?</summary>
*They may share training data, prompts, or reasoning errors. Require a reproducible failure or independently checked evidence rather than counting agreeing models.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 2: Why audit apparently clean outputs?</summary>
*Reviewing only reported findings measures false allegations but leaves missed defects invisible. Independent sampling of clean cases is needed to estimate recall.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 3: Why can additional review samples make oversight less useful?</summary>
*They may increase recall while producing enough false or duplicate allegations to consume the human review budget. Measure critical defects found per unit of review effort.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 4: What does weak-to-strong generalization leave unresolved?</summary>
*It does not recover all strong-model capability or prove intent learning and safe alignment. Generalization beyond the studied tasks and reliable supervision of hidden errors remain open.*
</details>

<details>
<summary>Quiz 5: Why separate verification from execution authority?</summary>
*A technically correct proposal can still exceed the user's authorization. An independent executor must enforce scope and permissions even when every critic approves.*
</details>
